A private volcanic island, chartered whole.

Kairo rises 1,180 metres out of open ocean and holds a single caldera lake at its crown. There is one house, one landing, one boat. For eleven days the island answers to twelve guests and no one else.

03 — The passage

Three days of open water.

Kairo has no airstrip. Guests join Tuhaka, a 38-metre expedition ketch, at Nuku'alofa and cross 640 nautical miles of the Tongan trench before the caldera breaks the horizon.

06 — The caldera

The island is still awake.

Kairo last vented in 1887. The caldera lake sits at 62 °C on its western edge and the whole massif is monitored continuously from Nuku'alofa. Six departures a year is not scarcity marketing — it is what the geology allows.
